Essays by Melanie Brown

Build for the day you're at fifty percent.

Most businesses are designed for peak energy. That's fragile. Dialogues is where I work out what durable looks like instead — in web architecture, in automation, and in AI that knows when to stop. It's the part nobody puts in the case study: what happens to a system when the person running it has a bad week.

Read Bluedobie Dialogues

Four threads, one argument

Capacity-Aware Design

The 50% Capacity Rule, margin as strategy, and why a workflow that needs your best day is not a workflow.

Governed AI

Automation that surfaces exceptions instead of inventing answers, and what it costs when a system has never been given permission to say "I don't know."

The Practical Web

Sites and stores that explain themselves. Information architecture built for the person visiting, not the person who already knows everything.

The Woods Behind Me

A memoir, in chapters. Bipolar disorder, military life, and fifteen years spent looking for stable ground. This is where the rest of it came from.

If your system only works when you feel good, it's not a system. It's a mood.

Dialogues lives on its own site at dialogues.bluedobiedev.com, where you can read every essay and subscribe by email — the systems writing and the memoir are separate letters, so you can take one or both.
